AI CFO

A monthly report that tells a small business owner what happened to their money, in sentences they can act on.

The problem

Most small business owners in Saudi Arabia can see their balance in a banking app, but not the story behind it: which costs grew, whether sales are slipping, how long the cash will last.

A finance manager would answer those questions every month. Most small businesses can't afford one, and accounting software gives them reports written for accountants.

[Add what you saw first-hand: a conversation with an owner, or the moment the idea started.]

Who it's for

Owners of shops, cafés, bakeries and service businesses who run the business themselves and check the numbers late at night, if at all.

[Name the first type of business you're testing with, and why.]

How it works

  1. 1

    Bring in the transactions

    The owner uploads a bank export or connects a spreadsheet. Nothing to re-enter.

  2. 2

    Sort and calculate

    Each line is sorted into sales, rent, salaries, suppliers and the rest, then the key figures are worked out: profit, margins, cash runway.

  3. 3

    Write the report

    Claude turns the figures into a short report: what happened, what changed, and what to look at next month.

Decisions I made

Sentences before charts

A chart shows what happened but doesn't say what to do about it. Every report opens with plain sentences; charts only back them up.

The math happens before the AI writes

Figures are calculated in code, and the model only explains them. That way a number in the report always matches the books.
